    Notes on Client Communication and Scope¶

    Effective communication with your client is essential to a successful project. Even though you are working independently, you are not expected to work without guidance. Timely check-ins can help prevent major issues later.

    When to Contact Your Client¶

    You should reach out to your client at key decision points, including:

    • Data Issues - If you encounter missing data, outliers, or unexpected data structures. If variables are unclear or require interpretation.
    • Defining the Analysis - To confirm key variable definitions (exposure, outcome, covariates) before proceeding with your analysis.
    • Preliminary Results - After generating initial results, to confirm your approach is aligned with the client's goals before refining/extending your analysis.
    • Scope Issues - If the project scope appears too large to reasonably complete within the 40 hours of consulting work.
    • Uncertainty or Confusion - If at any time you are unsure what the client is asking, or if your results are difficult to interpret or explain.

    A Note on Communication¶

    When in doubt, communicate early rather than after substantial work has been completed.

    • Clients may have deadlines that do not align with the course schedule. Communicate with your client to understand and meet these deadlines.
    • It is acceptable to share preliminary findings before the final report, especially if you would like clarification that you are on the right track.
    • Prioritize clear, correct, and interpretable analyses over complex models that are difficult to explain (unless otherwise asked).
    • Communicate early if you anticipate delays or challenges.

    Most of the major issues in past projects have resulted from lack of communication at key decision points.
